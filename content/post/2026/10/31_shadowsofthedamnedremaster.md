<game:Shadows of the Damned: Hella Remastered> is, more than anything else, a solid endorsement for [2023's Resident Evil 4 remake](game:Resident Evil 4 (2023)) by way of counter-example. Because the latter did much more than replace outdated art -- it re-engineered the aging original's control scheme, revised the game's *levels* around those new camera and locomotion systems, and even re-wrote segments of the campaign to try and match modern action-adventure expectations.

RE4's remake put in considerable effort to preserve the "spirit" of the original without feeling like a generations-old game. *Hella Remastered* reminded me how a generations-old game *does* feel.

Controls are a pretty big part of that "old" feeling. Back in 2011, <game:Shadows of the Damned> was a step or two beyond the "tank controls" of earlier <tag:Resident Evil>s, and that's where it remains today: Garcia still gets stuck in un-even terrain, the roll and quick-turn commands [still have baffling results]($SiteBaseURL$2011/06/26/shadows-of-the-damned-5/), aiming at moving targets is still an ordeal, the melee attack is still a cruel joke.

But even ignoring how frustrating the combat can be - and I mean, *really* ignoring it, as Lemon Hunter (easy-mode) and the attack-boosting Demonio Garcia costume (which makes him [look like a fuckin' idiot](https://static0.thegamerimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/wm/2024/10/sotddemonio.jpeg)) and progressive Red Gem upgrades absolutely trivialized most encounters - Shadows of the Damned's content isn't as remarkable as it was, anymore.

There's still a lot of fun in Garcia's foul-mouthed writing, and especially in Johnson's awkward-companion routine. But they're less shocking, less *edgy* than they once were; the edge has moved on. Now the thin-ness of the setting around them is more obvious.

And it's likewise for the game's variety of environments: most levels just blend together, while stand-outs like the library or the "Moor Pu Dekcuf" would be quickly forgotten if not for their clunky puzzles. By modern standards, the story's pacing stumbles pretty frequently on repetitive fight-after-fight sequences and on short-lived gimmicks.

The first side-scrolling schmup level felt fresh, but by the third one I was really ready for the game to be over.

I wouldn't say that Shadows of the Damned "aged poorly" in the same way that [grindhouse-genre stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grindhouse) often do (well, maybe Garcia's difficulty with written English has). I would say it's more an example of how far games have come. Like when <game:Red Faction: Guerrilla Re-Mars-tered> reminded me how [sparse open-worlds used to be]($SiteBaseURL$2018/08/20/get-your-ass-back-to-mars/), and replaying <game:The Saboteur> in 2019 reminded me of [life before fast travel]($SiteBaseURL$2019/09/08/some-fuckin-master-race/).

Sometimes looking back isn't all that pleasant.

**Better than**: <game:Killer Is Dead>  
**Not as good as**: <game:Resident Evil 4: Separate Ways>  
**Pretty much the same feeling as**: <game:Vanquish>'s 2017 Steam release.
