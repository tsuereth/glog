[Zelda](game:The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom - Nintendo Switch 2 Edition)'s world may be denser with interactivity, and [God of War](game:God of War Ragnarök)'s may be richer with gripping narrative and lore -- but even today, Tsuhima's **sense of place** is still remarkably distinct.

<game:Ghost of Tsushima: Director's Cut> is a great excuse to revisit it, and playing on my PC meaningfully upgraded the experience: fast travel was usually *instantaneous*, with no loading screen at all. (Runtime performance was solid too, I only noticed two or three framerate stutters through the whole campaign.)

Although I still, *still* cannot imagine having fun with the game on its default difficulty level, due very specifically to the "everything button" that is the R-trigger. My [previous gripe]($SiteBaseURL$2021/07/17/breath-of-the-mild-more-like-ninja-assassins-creed/):

> So you've killed some Mongols, their friends are still attacking you, you need to switch stances to fight them, you press R2, *Jin leans down to pick up loot and gets hit in the back like an idiot*.  This happens *all the damn time*.

... remains a serious issue, mitigated only by turning down the game's combat difficulty.

And, yeah, the main story of Jin disappointing his samurai uncle is still kinda dull.

But roaming the world, doing errands and collecting collectibles, is such an immersively *serene* time. The way in which Ghost of Tsushima's beautiful wind and smoke and wildlife effects are designed *into* your journeys around the map ... man. I'm still impressed.

And! The Director's Cut adds Iki Island, a new, fourth map region with its own story and activities.

Some of the new enemy types are *annoying as shit*. Singing shamans who give their friends near-impenetrable defense, and multi-weapon brutes who can shift their combat stance just like Jin. I didn't like fighting these guys very much.

Iki's new quests, though, have a tighter - and more personal - narrative focus than the original game's. It delves into young Jin's relationship with his father, and how dear old Dad's take on "honor" contrasts with adult Jin's newfound sense of right and wrong. This story feels less flatly save-the-world, and more about Jin's own character arc.

It's also refreshing to engage with the ~~pirate~~ "raider" NPCs who actively *dislike* the samurai. They're a fun change of pace from Tsushima's *"Lord Sakai"* this and *"thank you samurai"* that.

Plus, horse armor! No, not [that kind](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Oblivion:Horse_Armor_Pack); Iki Island's quests include unlocking **combat armor for your horse**, and a charge ability, finally allowing you to stampede directly into those Mongols that keep hogging the road. On top of being a fun gameplay twist, this new ability makes the horse feel like a more integral part of Jin's story all-up.

But most importantly, among Iki's new optional activities are shrines where you play flute songs to the local wildlife. And you can pet the cats.

![]($SiteBaseURL$ghost-of-tsushima-directors-cut_pet-the-cat.jpg){width=960 height=540}

Iki Island isn't like, a total overhaul of the core Tsushima gameplay formula, but it does show Sucker Punch successfully building on their formula's strengths. It has me hoping even more for Sony to [quit fuckin' around](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/03/sony-wont-bring-any-more-single-player-playstation-games-to-pc/) and let me play <game:Ghost of Yotei> without paying $600+ for the privilege.
