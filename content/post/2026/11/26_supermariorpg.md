<game:Super Mario RPG: Legend of the Seven Stars> is one of my ["cornerstone" games]($SiteBaseURL$2019/10/06/blast-to-the-past/); some of my fondest, oldest (*circa 1996!*) gaming memories come from it. And like <game:Chrono Trigger (2018)>, playing SMRPG again **30 years later** was bound to be a mixed experience -- but this remake's ... mixture ... is a little more dubious.

On paper, <game:Super Mario RPG (2023)> *sounds* like it's really [putting in the work](https://www.ign.com/wikis/super-mario-rpg-switch-remake/Super_Mario_RPG_Remake_New_Features_and_Differences) to make an <platform:SNES> game feel like new: Real 3D graphics! Re-arranged soundtrack! New combat features! New quality-of-life enhancements!

In practice, though, I didn't appreciate the bulk of that work.

Environment art and characters are rendered in real-time 3D, sure; but the 1996 original's pixel art was *so detailed and expressive*, I wouldn't call the new models an "improvement." Granting that I've got some hefty nostalgia bias, I just don't think the remake's 3D models add any detail or personality to what the 2D sprites already had.

In fact, I'd say the new animated cutscenes *take away* some personality from the original, which portrayed most story moments in the same overhead isometric perspective as gameplay. When the remake cuts to a zooming, whooshing camera to show those moments - especially when receiving Star Pieces, against a bizarrely blank white background - it feels disconnected, separate from the rest of the game.

The soundtrack, likewise, is "new" and sounds more like real musical instruments than the SNES's synthetic ones -- but it's little more than a modern translation of the distinctive and excellent tracks that [Yoko Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoko_Shimomura#Composition) already made, 30 years ago. Again, nostalgia bias, but after listening to the old and new music side-by-side I tend to prefer the originals.

And of all the old-school games to get an Easy Mode, Super Mario RPG might be the *last* one where you'd ever need it. The game was already plenty easy!

![]($SiteBaseURL$super-mario-rpg-2023_such-a-crybaby.jpg){width=960 height=540}

Alright, alright. I can admit that I'm being extra hard on this remake, the original having been so close to my blocky, pixel-art heart.

And the new stuff definitely isn't all bad. Many of the remake's quality-of-life improvements are extremely welcome: fast-travel from the menu, party-switching during combat, **autosaves**! I certainly appreciate being able to play SMRPG with these modern design conveniences.

New combat buffs and team-attack abilities, powered by successful action-command timing, are pretty unnecessary - like I said, the game was easy already - but do add a bit of variety to otherwise-repetitive minion battles.

I did find myself wishing that movement controls had got a fresh look, since despite all the 3D-ification, and despite the invention of *analog control sticks*, your field movement is still restricted to 8 directions.

... Okay, *now* I'm done complaining about the remake.

Having said that, the original game's core design and narrative - fondly though I remember them - are sadly showing their age. Non-boss fights *do* get repetitive, since so many special abilities have unreasonably high ~~magic~~ flower point costs, turning most fights into the same sequence of normal attack after normal attack after normal attack. Field maps - including towns - are awkwardly small, a relic of the handful of megabytes Square was working with at the time.

The story, although chock-full of memorable characters and locations, is **short** -- what Mario and crew *do* in each location is fairly limited. In my memory, Super Mario RPG had a seemingly endless supply of optional activities and hidden secrets; in reality, everything other than the [absurd 100-jump challenge](https://www.ign.com/wikis/super-mario-rpg-switch-remake/How_to_Perform_100_Super_Jumps) can be done in [15 hours or less](https://howlongtobeat.com/game/130456).

But! There are *also* aspects of the original, ancient game that hold up really well. Like the time-sensitive action commands for attacking and blocking, which feel *less bullshit* than in subsequent <tag:Mario & Luigi> installments (or in <game:Clair Obscur: Expedition 33>), because getting a few wrong doesn't completely screw you over.

And the writing - or maybe I should say, the localization - for main characters is still brimming with wit and charm. In retrospect, significant parts of Bowser's and ~~Princess Toadstool~~ Peach's current-day characterizations are owed to the personalities invented here.

Super Mario RPG's remake succeeded in reminding me of what I loved about one of my favorite SNES games, and it did so with minimal old-school frustrations. It didn't improve upon the original quite like I think it *could* have, but, I guess not every remake can be as ambitious as <game:Resident Evil 4 (2023)>.

**Better than**: <game:Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story>, <game:Penny Arcade's On the Rain-Slick Precipice of Darkness 3>  
**Not as good as**: <game:Chrono Trigger (2018)>  
**Here's hoping**: that [Ocarina of Time remake](game:The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (2026)) puts in a bit more effort.
