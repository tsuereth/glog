Not since ~~Sonic All-Stars Racing~~ er, ~~Sonic Racing All-Stars?~~ ... *<game:Sonic & All-Stars Racing Transformed>* have my attempts to use a game's multiplayer features been so absolutely confounded.

First, each of my co-op buddies and myself had to - separately - play enough of the <game:Monster Hunter Wilds> campaign to *enable* multiplayer. This took about an hour's worth of cinematic cutscenes, non-interactive story scenes, combat tutorials, **a boss battle**, MORE cutscenes, MORE non-interactive story scenes; it was a whole "thing." And an ambiguous thing to boot, only documented in [fan-written guides](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3435493455).

Next we had to try and parse all the different *forms* of multiplayer, which are ... a lot. It turns out that creating a [Lobby](https://manual.capcom.com/mhwilds/en/steam/page/7/1), giving that Lobby ID to friends, and asking them to join that Lobby, only brings friends into your Base Camp -- not quests or out-of-camp gameplay.

Inviting friends into a [Link Party](https://manual.capcom.com/mhwilds/en/steam/page/7/3) only enables *chat features* -- still no gameplay.

Now, within a Link Party, creating an "Environment Link" shares the world outside Base Camp with you and your friends; but *not* in story quests. In fact, Environment Link *blocks* everyone in it from starting a new quest, until and unless one leaves the Environment Link.

The feature that finally achieves multiplayer co-operative quests is - starting with an existing Lobby or Link Party (but not an Environment Link!) - [Joining Another Player's Quest](https://manual.capcom.com/mhwilds/en/steam/page/7/2). One player must start the quest solo, and then other players can join the in-progress quest.

I'm not even going to bother detailing the UI hell of nested menus involved in these options. It's, well, **wild**.

Of course... you can only join another player's active quest if *your own* story progress has unlocked that quest, or if you've already done it.

Also, *not all quests* are joinable, only ones with combat. Most narrative-focused quests - like NPC conversations or traveling to a new map - cannot be joined by another player (but are still blocked by an Environment Link).

So to recap, you can't play together from the start; you can only play together in certain quests; and you must have a quest unlocked to join it. Meaning that the only way to play through the story "together" is for everyone to do non-multiplayer story sequences separately, then join up for share-able hunting quests, then break for the next story sequence, and so on.

But my anguish regarding its impenetrable multiplayer is only part of why I lost interest in Monster Hunter Wilds. Fact is, the gameplay itself (with or without friends) is absolutely not for me.

Early combat is incredibly tedious: monsters take so much damage, and require so much repetitive chasing, I felt **bored** more than anything else. And with all the resource-collection and equipment-upgrade mechanics that NPCs keep yammering on about, I can tell that the path forward is just going to be more of the same with bigger numbers.

Approaching Monster Hunter Wilds as a co-op [friendslop-ey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop) experience didn't work, but I don't think that's because Capcom failed to implement fun multiplayer features. I mean, *they did fail*, but I don't think they were aiming for a full co-op experience in the first place.

I think they might've been aiming for an experience that existing <tag:Monster Hunter> fans, who've been grinding through gear upgrades in repetitive hunting missions for 20 years already, and who might actually understand the game's labyrinth of menus, could play *and replay* together.

Whether their fanbase considers this a success, or not, I couldn't say. I'm not in it.
